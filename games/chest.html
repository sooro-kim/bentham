The Money in the Chest

Can saving ever hurt a nation? The young Bentham said no. Later he said yes, if the money is put "into a chest" and kept there. Forecast what happens year by year. Then advise the government how much new money to issue.

The rules of this economy

Capacity output F = 1,000 (what every willing worker can make in a year). Savers put aside a share s = 0.15 of income. A share h of their savings goes into a chest; the rest is lent and spent by borrowers. The government may issue new money M.

Next year's spending: D = Y − s·h·Y + M.
Compare it with what capacity output costs at last year's prices: g = D ÷ (F × P).

If g < 1, the price level becomes P × √g and output becomes F × √g. If g ≥ 1, output is at capacity and the price level becomes P × g.

A simplified, stated version of the argument in Hunt & Lautzenheiser, pp. 132–33. Hunt reports no numbers for this argument; the numbers here are ours. Prices are shown as an index (P = 1 at the start is shown as 100).
