Jeremy Bentham

A lawyer who never practised and wrote for more than sixty years. Hunt calls his utilitarian philosophy the base on which neoclassical economics was later built. His life comes in five short chapters. Each one ends with a question, and the next chapter opens when you answer it.

Born
15 February 1748, Houndsditch, London
Died
6 June 1832, Queen Square Place, Westminster, aged 84
Training
Westminster School; The Queen's College, Oxford (BA 1763, MA 1766); called to the bar 1769, but never practised
Major works
A Fragment on Government (1776)
An Introduction to the Principles of Morals and Legislation (printed 1780; published 1789)
Defence of Usury (1787)
Panopticon; or, The Inspection House (1791)
Plan of Parliamentary Reform (printed 1817)
Coined
The word “utilitarian” (1781)
Argued with
Blackstone, on the laws of England; Adam Smith, on the legal maximum rate of interest

Based on Hunt & Lautzenheiser, History of Economic Thought, 3rd ed., ch. 6, pp. 125–134. Biographical details not in Hunt (family, schooling, Blackstone, Russia, the Panopticon, James Mill, reform, the Auto-icon) follow the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry “Jeremy Bentham” and Wikipedia, checked 22 October 2026.
