Sources & credits

Where the content, the quotations, and the pictures on this site come from.

Main text

  • Hunt, E. K., and Mark Lautzenheiser. History of Economic Thought: A Critical Perspective. 3rd ed. Armonk, NY: M.E. Sharpe, 2011.
    • Chapter 6, “Rationalistic Subjectivism: The Economics of Bentham, Say, and Senior,” pp. 125–152: the basis for every page and game on this site. Bentham is treated on pp. 129–134, the social origins of utility theory on pp. 125–129, Say on pp. 134–138, and Senior on pp. 138–151.
    • About: pp. 125–134.
    • Timeline: pp. 129–139, and ch. 7, pp. 153–154 (the Combination Act of 1799, machine breaking from 1811).
    • Critics & Legacy: pp. 125–137 and 144–149 (Hobbes, Say, Senior, and Hunt's own critique on p. 134).
    • Quotes: pp. 130–133 for the ten passages quoted from Hunt; the notes also draw on pp. 137, 142, 144–145, and 147.
    • Key Ideas: pp. 125–134 (the five features of capitalism, p. 129; Hobbes, p. 126), with pp. 135, 137, and 144–145 for Say and Senior.
    • Model: pp. 130 (the seven circumstances), 133 (the labourer and the richest man), and 132–133 and 137 (saving, the chest, and Say's law).
    • The Felicific Calculus: pp. 129–130 and 144–145. The Labourer and the Richest Man: pp. 133–134 and 144–145. The Money in the Chest: pp. 132–133 and 137–138. The Tribunal of Utility: pp. 131–147. The Examination: pp. 125–147.
  • The numbers in the models and games are ours, chosen to make Bentham's arguments visible, and each page says so. The one number that is Bentham's is his guess that a man with 50,000 times the labourer's income has at most five, and more likely two, times the pleasure (p. 133). The formulas are listed at the top of js/econ.js.
  • Hunt's own sources for the Bentham passages, cited on the Quotes page by volume and page: Jeremy Bentham's Economic Writings, 3 vols., ed. W. Stark (London: Allen and Unwin, 1954); and An Introduction to the Principles of Morals and Legislation, in A Bentham Reader, ed. M. P. Mack (New York: Pegasus, 1969).

Outside sources for verified facts

Hunt gives few biographical details. Dates and events in Bentham's life that are not in Hunt, and the quotations from later writers, come only from the sources below, checked on 22 October 2026. Where the two reference works disagree (the years of Bentham's Oxford degrees), the site follows the Stanford Encyclopedia: BA 1763, MA 1766.

  • “Jeremy Bentham.” Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bentham/
  • “Jeremy Bentham.” Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Bentham
  • Marx, Karl. Capital, vol. I (1867), ch. 24, §5 and its footnote on Bentham. Marxists Internet Archive: https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch24.htm
  • Mill, John Stuart. Utilitarianism, ch. 2. First published in Fraser's Magazine, 1861; as a book, 1863.
  • Jevons, W. S. The Theory of Political Economy (1871), ch. II, “Theory of Pleasure and Pain.”
  • Edgeworth, F. Y. Mathematical Psychics (1881): the “hedonimeter.”
  • Robbins, Lionel. “Interpersonal Comparisons of Utility: A Comment.” Economic Journal 48, no. 192 (December 1938): 635–641.
  • Rawls, John. A Theory of Justice (1971).
  • Kahneman, Daniel, Peter Wakker, and Rakesh Sarin. “Back to Bentham? Explorations of Experienced Utility.” Quarterly Journal of Economics 112, no. 2 (1997). doi:10.1162/003355397555235
