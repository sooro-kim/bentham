2 · The labourer and the richest man

Utility of an income y is yθ. Bentham's guess fixes θ: 50,000 times the income gives R times the pleasure, so θ = ln R / ln 50,000 (Hunt p. 133). Ten labourers earn 20 each; one rich man earns 1,000,000, which is 50,000 times as much. A share t of his income is spread equally among the labourers. Bentham's limit, the inducement to labour, is modelled as a loss of output: every income is multiplied by (1 − k·t²).

3 · The money in the chest

The town begins with spending of 100 at prices of 1.00, enough to employ everyone (capacity 100). Each year a share s of income is saved and a share h of the saving goes “into a chest” instead of being lent out. The government may add new money M each year. Next year's spending = income − s·h·income + M. If spending falls short of capacity at current prices, prices and output both fall; if it exceeds capacity, prices rise (Hunt pp. 132–33, 137).
