Jeremy Bentham

— the accountant of pleasure and pain —

1748 · 1832

A lawyer who never practised and spent sixty years trying to make law and government into a science. He reduced every human motive to pleasure and pain, proposed to measure them, and so laid the philosophical floor that neoclassical economics was later built on. Then he followed his own logic to conclusions his heirs did not want: that a pound does more good in a poor man's hand, and that saving can leave men out of work.

One question before anything else

The question this course asks of every writer

Does market exchange harmonize our interests, or conceal a conflict between them?

Bentham gives two answers. Young, he trusted the market to do the most good. Older, he saw two ways it could fail: money hoarded instead of spent, and wealth piled where it gives the least pleasure. By the end of this site you should be able to say why one principle led him to both.
